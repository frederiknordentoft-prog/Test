# Tidal Memory

*An algorithmic philosophy*

Water keeps a record of every force that moved it, and almost none of that record is visible. Tidal Memory is a generative practice that makes the record visible. Forces are never drawn. Only the evidence of their passing is drawn: thousands of hairline strands, each one the path of a single particle that let itself be carried. The image is the sum of those paths and nothing else. It is the product of deep computational expertise, a meticulously crafted algorithm in which every constant has been weighed against its neighbours until the system breathes on its own.

The current is built as a stream function, a scalar landscape of layered simplex noise, and the particles follow its curl. That choice is the movement's first principle. A curl field is divergence-free: there are no drains and no sources, so matter is never created or destroyed in the flow. Strands may crowd into fast jets or thin out over slack water, but they can never collapse into a point. The landscape is folded into itself by domain warping, so that large gyres carry smaller eddies in their arms, and it is dimmed by a slow envelope that opens calm basins where almost nothing moves. Each octave is rotated against the one before it, a small piece of painstaking care that removes the grid from the noise and leaves only the water.

Time enters as a tide, not as a clock. The field is sampled at two moments and the system swings between them on a slow cosine while the image accumulates, so a strand drawn early and a strand drawn late do not quite agree on where the current runs. Their disagreement is the texture: interleaved threads that cross at shallow angles, the way rope fibres or wet hair cross. A steady drift can be laid over the whole field to stretch round gyres into long streaming bands, the way a real current stretches an eddy.

Light is additive. Every segment adds a little to the pixels beneath it and nothing subtracts, so brightness is a measure of how much water passed through a place, scaled by how fast it went. Fast jets glow; slack water stays dark. Each strand is tapered at both ends by its own lifespan, rising out of the dark and sinking back into it, so nothing begins or ends with a hard edge. Colour is assigned by the water mass a particle is born into: most strands belong to a cold body that runs from deep blue to surface light, and a minority carry a warm thread through it. The ratio is a parameter. The restraint is not.

Every seed is a different sea under the same physics. The same seed always produces the same sea, stroke for stroke, because every random choice is drawn from seeded streams that never share state. The parameters are few and each one names a physical quantity: the scale of the swirls, the depth of turbulence, the strength of the fold, the drift, the tide, the number of particles, how long each lives, how far it moves per step, how long the plate is exposed, how much light each stroke carries. Turning any one of them should feel like adjusting a real instrument. The finished piece should read as the result of countless iterations by someone at the very top of the field: a master-level implementation where nothing is decoration and every strand is a measurement.
